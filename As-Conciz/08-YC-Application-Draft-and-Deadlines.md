## 3. The One-Minute Founder Video — Structure

Per current, verified YC guidance, the video answers three plain questions, in plain language, no buzzwords:

1. **What do you do?** (the one/two-sentence answer from §2 above, spoken naturally)
2. **Who are you, and why are you the right people to build this?** — Your Alpha.Kore track record (11-person studio, real shipped client/product work) and your build-by-doing pattern are genuine, specific answers here — use them plainly rather than generically claiming "we're passionate about AI."
3. **What have you built / what's your traction?** — Show, don't summarize. If you have a working prototype, demo it briefly on screen rather than describing it.

Practical notes from current guidance: webcam is fine, good lighting matters more than production value, all founders should appear if there's a team, one or two takes, under 60 seconds, and do not read from a script word-for-word — it should sound like a person talking, not a pitch.

## 4. Rapid-Fire Interview Prep (if shortlisted)

Current guides consistently report the same interview pattern: partners interrupt long answers, ask the same core questions fast, and probe with follow-ups. Practice one-sentence answers to:

- What does your company do?
- Who is your user, specifically (not "everyone")?
- How do you make money?
- What's your traction, in numbers?
- Why will customers switch from Copilot/Comet/their existing habits to this?
- What happens if Microsoft or Google builds this natively into Windows/Chrome?
- Why you, specifically, for this problem?
- What's your churn? (if you have any paying users by interview time)

Know your real numbers cold before the interview — MRR, active users, retention, whatever you actually have. Do not estimate live in the room.

## 5. Known Weak Points to Get Ahead Of (be ready, don't hide from these)

- **Hardware-precedent skepticism:** partners have almost certainly seen the Humane/Rabbit pattern discussed publicly. Your strongest answer is the one already in §2 above — you're explicitly not a hardware company, and you can say so with the pivot as evidence of judgment, not indecision.
- **"Why not just use Copilot/Comet" objection:** your answer is the friction difference (app-switch vs. one existing button) — make sure your demo actually proves this is meaningfully faster, not just different.
- **Solo-or-small-team question:** if applying without the full Alpha.Kore team formally attached as cofounders, be ready to answer "why solo" or "why this team" directly and specifically, per current guidance on how YC frames that question.
